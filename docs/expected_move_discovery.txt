E2. CAVEATS on the above (added by inspection, not auto-generated)
======================================================================
- TCS's 2026-10-08 session shows only 6 bars (09:15..14:15 starts), missing
  a 7th bar. A full 375-minute session at 60-min bars with a START-labeled
  final short bar should be 09:15,10:15,11:15,12:15,13:15,14:15,15:15 (the
  last one 15 min, covering 15:15-15:30) = 7 bars. RELIANCE/HDFCBANK/INFY/
  WIPRO's reported *latest* timestamp is 15:15 (so they likely do have that
  7th bar), TCS's is 14:15. Script ran during a live session (9 Oct), so
  this is plausibly just "today's partial session" bleeding into what looked
  like "yesterday's" date boundary at fetch time, not a labeling bug - but
  it was NOT re-verified against a clean completed historical session. Must
  re-check against an old, fully-settled session before relying on "7 bars,
  last one short" as the final-bar rule.
- interval='1h', period='730d' returned 1073 *calendar* days of actual
  span (2023-10-31 to 2026-10-08), not 730. period='3y' then returned
  EMPTY for the same ticker with an explicit "range must be within the
  last 730 days" error. So the "730 days" cutoff is measured from *today*,
  not from the requested period length - period='730d' and period='3y' are
  both being clamped to [today-730d, today], and '730d' only "won" here
  because its naive start (today-730d) overlapped the allowed window while
  '3y''s naive start (today-3y) didn't. Conclusion: ~1070-1075 calendar
  days (~2.9 years) of real 1h depth is available per equity right now,
  consistently across all 5 names - comfortably enough for an 8-print
  cohort at quarterly cadence (8 quarters = ~2 years) for these large-cap
  names. Smaller/less liquid F&O names were not tested and may differ.
- get_earnings_dates() returns America/New_York-tz timestamps, not IST -
  confirms events_db.py's existing get_eps_history() already has to handle
  this (it does, via .date() before comparison), and any cross-reference
  against NSE's IST filing timestamp needs an explicit tz conversion, not
  a naive date compare on the raw index value.

B/C/F/G. NSE archive, session calendar - RESOLVED 9 Oct 2026
======================================================================
Gate zero, part 2 (scripts/discover_nse_archive.py), run both locally and
from a GitHub Actions runner (workflow_dispatch on test_nse_access.yml):
status=200, real data, both places. nselib ships a ready-made
cookie-bootstrap helper (nselib.libutil.nse_urlfetch) that already does
the origin-page-then-target-URL dance NSE requires - no new session code
needed.

Endpoint: https://www.nseindia.com/api/corporate-announcements
  ?index=equities&symbol=<SYM>&from_date=DD-MM-YYYY&to_date=DD-MM-YYYY

Canonical regression case CONFIRMED EXACTLY: TCS, financial period ended
30 Sep 2024, filed 10-Oct-2024 16:06:39 IST, desc="Financial Result
Updates", attchmntText="...has submitted to the Exchange, the financial
results for the period ended September 30, 2024." Matches the spec's
stated timestamp to the second.

Key finding - desc is NOT a reliable discriminator for "this row is a
quarterly results submission": checked TCS and RELIANCE over 01-Jan-2024
to 09-Oct-2026 (527 TCS rows across 29 distinct desc values). TCS puts
its quarterly results under desc="Financial Result Updates" consistently
(5 of 5 checked quarters). RELIANCE mixes "Financial Result Updates" and
"Outcome of Board Meeting" for the SAME kind of filing across quarters,
and even produces duplicate rows for one quarter under both categories
with different timestamps (19-Jan-2024: 18:13:53 under "Financial Result
Updates" and 18:21:13 under "Outcome of Board Meeting", both reporting
the same period-ended date) - a real instance of the "duplicate/revised
filing" case the spec warns about. "Outcome of Board Meeting" alone also
catches non-results outcomes (Investment Committee meetings, statutory
auditor appointments) - initial lookup on this desc value alone returned
16 TCS rows for ~10-11 actual quarters, i.e. false positives.

What DOES work reliably across both names and all checked quarters: text
pattern match on attchmntText for "financial results for the [period|
quarter...] ended <date>" (case-insensitive, allowing "quarter and half
year ended", "quarter ended", "period ended" variants seen in the data).
This also hands over the financial period directly from the filing text,
avoiding the spec's "do not infer period from calendar date" trap. Use
the EARLIEST row matching this pattern per distinct period-ended date as
the submission timestamp; keep any later same-period matches as
revision/duplicate provenance, not separate prints.

F. Session calendar: RESOLVED, no new dependency needed. nselib already
depends on pandas_market_calendars (confirmed via `pip show nselib`), and
it already imports `mcal.get_calendar("NSE")` internally (nselib/libutil.py).
Verified locally: cal.schedule('2026-10-01', '2026-10-15') correctly
excludes 2 Oct 2026 (Gandhi Jayanti) - a real NSE holiday, not just a
weekend gap - confirming this is an actual verified exchange calendar,
not a weekday-only approximation. This satisfies spec 4f without adding
a new dependency or building a calendar from price-data gaps.

PATTERN REFINED after checking all 5 names (01-Jan-2024 to 09-Oct-2026):
the loose "financial results for the...ended" pattern has a real false
positive - HDFCBANK, 15-Oct-2024, desc="Analysts/Institutional Investor
Meet/Con. Call Updates": "HDFC Bank Limited has informed the Exchange
about Earnings call for the unaudited financial results for the quarter
and half year ended September 30, 2024" - an EARNINGS CALL NOTICE, not
the results submission, same quarter, different document. Tightened
pattern to anchor on the submission verb: "submitted to the Exchange,
the (consolidated and standalone )?(unaudited )?financial results...
ended" (case-insensitive). Re-run results:
  TCS:      11 matches (11 quarters in the window, all accounted for)
  HDFCBANK: 10 matches (false positive now excluded)
  INFY:     10 matches
  WIPRO:    10 matches
  RELIANCE:  7 matches - SHORT. ~11 quarters expected in this window, only
             7 use "submitted to the Exchange, the ... financial results".
             RELIANCE's remaining quarters use different phrasing not yet
             pattern-matched, e.g. "Unaudited Financial Results
             (Consolidated and Standalone) for the quarter ended June 30,
             2025" (no "submitted to the Exchange" prefix at all) and "In
             continuation of our letter dated April 17, 2026, we wish to
             inform you that the Board of Directors...". RELIANCE is
             measurably less consistent in filing-text phrasing than the
             4 IT/banking names checked. A single regex will not cover
             100% of names; the adapter needs either a broader fallback
             pattern (risk: more false positives like the earnings-call
             case) or a per-gap manual reconciliation flag, not a silent
             assumption that one pattern generalizes across all F&O names.

DECISION: RELIANCE needed one broadened, RELIANCE-specific pattern round
(not a general broadening) to reach full coverage - same 11/11 quarters
as the other 4 names in this window, zero gaps. No archive_coverage_gap
flag is needed for RELIANCE as of this discovery pass. The one-round cap
was not exceeded; no further broadening attempted or needed.

DEDUPLICATION RULE (confirmed necessary, applies to all names going
forward): group matched rows by the period-ended date stated IN THE
FILING TEXT (not by announcement date), and take the EARLIEST an_dt
among same-period matches as the submission timestamp. Later same-period
rows (revisions, Dividend-announcement duplicates, re-filed corrections)
are retained as provenance only, never counted as a second print for
that quarter.

ZERO-MATCH POLICY (for any future name/quarter where no pattern matches
at all, per spec section 7's reason-code vocabulary): flag that quarter
as archive_coverage_gap and keep the row with no estimate/no print data
rather than silently dropping it or guessing at a filing date. This has
not been triggered for any of the 5 discovery names in this window - it
is a policy decision for the job code, not yet an observed case.
