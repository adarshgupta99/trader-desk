F&O CORRELATION MATRIX - SPEC FOR CLAUDE CODE
Repo: trader-desk (Streamlit app). Uses fno_sectors.json already in the repo.
Read CLAUDE.md first. Do the steps in order. Stop after each step and show output.

======================================================================
1. GOAL
======================================================================
Correlation between every pair of the 213 F&O stocks, for any lookback
the user picks. In the app: pick a stock, see the names most and least
correlated with it, coloured by sector. The full 213x213 matrix is
stored and queryable but NOT drawn as a grid (about 22,000 pairs, nobody
can read that).

======================================================================
2. NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES
======================================================================
  1. Correlate LOG RETURNS, never prices. Prices all trend together and
     every pair looks correlated. Returns show real co-movement.
       r_t = ln(P_t / P_{t-1})
  2. Never use daily bars for lookbacks under 60 days. 20 daily points
     is noise. 20 days of 15-min bars is about 500 points.
  3. Handle stale bars the same way everywhere (see section 6) and
     document the choice in the code and in CLAUDE.md.
  4. 15 minutes is the FLOOR for bar size. Do not go finer, even though
     Yahoo serves 1-minute bars. Correlations from very fine bars are
     biased toward zero by asynchronous trading (the Epps effect): two
     stocks that genuinely move together look weakly correlated simply
     because they do not print in the same minute. It is severe for the
     illiquid end of the F&O list, and no stale-bar policy fixes it,
     because thin-but-nonzero bars pass every filter.

======================================================================
3. DATA TIERS (measured 6 Oct 2026, keep exactly)
======================================================================
  Lookback            Bars used            Why
  ------------------  -------------------  ---------------------------
  1 to 60 days        15-minute            Yahoo's 15m limit is 60 days
  > 60 days           daily                existing daily cache

Tier choice lives in ONE function:
  pick_tier(lookback_days) -> "15m" | "1d"
Boundaries: 1 -> "15m", 60 -> "15m", 61 -> "1d".
A lookback of 0 or negative raises an error.

Adarsh's decision (6 Oct 2026): the range starts at 1 day, not 5. A
1-day lookback is about 24 returns, which clears min_periods and
produces a real but noisy number. It is not rejected. The guard is
visibility, not refusal: n_obs is in the results table (section 8), so
a thin lookback is evident in the output rather than hidden behind a
number that looks like any other.

Two tiers, not three. The 1-minute tier was dropped on 6 Oct 2026 for
the reason in rule 4 of section 2, plus its operating cost: ~0.6 GB per
6 months, pruning logic, and a daily job that MUST run because 1m
history older than 7 days cannot be refetched from Yahoo at any price.
See section 11.

15m, not 1h, for the middle tier. Measured, not assumed: 15m returns
about 1,445 bars over 60 days against 1h's ~360 — four times the
observations over the same window, and both are capped at the same 60
days of practical reach here. Do not mix the two.

Never compare returns from different bar sizes in one number. A 30-day
correlation is from 15m bars only.

======================================================================
5. STORAGE
======================================================================
SQLite file OUTSIDE the repo, default path ~/trader_desk_data/intraday.db
(configurable through an env var TRADER_DESK_DATA_DIR in .env). The repo
must not contain bar data. Add the path to .gitignore anyway.

Table bars:
  symbol TEXT, interval TEXT ('15m'), ts TEXT (ISO, IST),
  open REAL, high REAL, low REAL, close REAL, volume INTEGER,
  PRIMARY KEY (symbol, interval, ts)
Insert with INSERT OR IGNORE so refetching overlapping windows is safe.
Index on (interval, ts).
(interval stays in the key and the schema even with one tier stored, so
adding another bar size later does not need a migration.)

Size: MEASURED by step 0 on 6 Oct 2026, in this exact schema with this
index, after VACUUM — 306,280 rows for the full 213 over 60 days is
50.6 MB, at 165 bytes a row. That is the whole tier, not a yearly rate.
The earlier ~40 MB estimate in this spec was about 25% low; 165 bytes a
row is the figure to size against.

Daily bars: REVISED 6 Oct 2026. This spec said they stay in the existing
cache and must not be duplicated. They now go into the same bars table
under interval='1d', and here is why. market_cache.db's price_cache is
keyed by (ticker, period, interval) and prices.get_price_history fetches
ONE ticker per call with its own freshness window — it was built for the
dozen curated instruments, not for 213 symbols. Going through it means
213 sequential Yahoo calls on a cold cache, against one bulk download of
all 213 in 6.0s (measured, step 0). It is both slower and less polite,
and it would put the correlation feature's reads on a different code
path from the 15m tier for no benefit. 213 x 250 daily rows is ~9 MB, so
the duplication costs nothing, and daily bars are as refetchable as 15m
ones. prices.py keeps owning the curated instruments; nothing there
changes.

Retention: keep the newest 65 SESSIONS of 15m bars and prune anything
older. More serves no tier, because the 15m tier only answers lookbacks
up to 60 days and Yahoo serves that entire window on demand.

Sessions, not calendar days — corrected 6 Oct 2026 when the first run of
the job deleted bars it had just fetched. A 60-session lookback spans 83
calendar days (58 sessions from 14 Jul to 5 Oct 2026), so a 65-calendar-
day cutoff throws away a quarter of the window the longest lookback
needs. Counting sessions makes retention mean the same thing as the
lookback it exists to serve, and holidays stop mattering. The daily tier
keeps 800 sessions, covering the 500-day maximum with headroom.

That last point is the whole prize from dropping 1m, so do not design
around it being otherwise: the maximum lookback the tier serves equals
Yahoo's retention for it, so stored bars are DISPOSABLE. The DB can
always be rebuilt from scratch, a missed fetch day costs nothing, and no
history is ever stranded. The 1m tier had the opposite property.

Rollup CSVs in the repo: DROPPED 6 Oct 2026. They existed only so a
Streamlit Cloud deploy with no SQLite file could still answer. Adarsh
confirmed the repo is private and the app runs on localhost only, so
there is no deploy without the DB beside it. The rollups would have been
an expiring binary re-committed forever, for a fallback that never
fires. If the app is ever deployed, revisit this — do not reintroduce it
speculatively.

Table corr_cache (inside the same DB): OPTIONAL. Schema, if built:
  tier, lookback_days, asof_date, symbol_a, symbol_b, corr, n_obs
  PRIMARY KEY (tier, lookback_days, asof_date, symbol_a, symbol_b)
Store each pair once (symbol_a < symbol_b alphabetically) and read both
directions through a helper.

MEASURED 6 Oct 2026, on the real 306,280 bars with their real gaps:
the worst case this feature has — 60 days, 1,450 rows, all 213 columns,
all 22,578 pairs — correlates in 0.12s, plus 0.09s for the n_obs matrix.
Pandas does take its pairwise path (95.09% non-null density) but
implements it in Cython, not a Python loop. At 20 days it is 0.03s.
So corr_cache is an OPTIMISATION, not load-bearing. A per-process memo
on (tier, lookback, asof) is enough for the UI; a SQLite cache table
would be storing a tenth of a second. Build the table only if a later
feature needs correlations without loading bars first.

The real cost is getting the bars into shape, not correlating them —
1.45s against 0.12s, twelve times the correlation:
  read 306,280 rows from sqlite   0.54s
  parse timestamps + stale policy 0.84s
  pivot to wide                   0.07s
So intraday_store.py must (a) read ONLY the requested window with a
WHERE on ts, not the whole table, and (b) store ts in a form that sorts
and filters as text and parses with a fixed format, so no row is parsed
that the lookback does not need. Optimise there, not in the maths.

6. COMPUTE
======================================================================
File: analytics/correlation.py

  1. Load bars for the tier and lookback window for all symbols.
  2. Build a wide table: rows = timestamps, columns = symbols, values =
     close price.
  3. STALE BAR POLICY. Settled 6 Oct 2026 by measurement, not by
     preference. Across 13 F&O names spanning the liquidity range,
     60 days of 15m bars, 18,709 bars total: only 9 had Volume == 0,
     which is 0.048%. All 9 were the 15:15 bar. They clustered on three
     dates (6 Aug, 24 Aug, 23 Sep) rather than on particular symbols,
     the price moved inside every one of them, and the most liquid name
     in the sample (RELIANCE) had the most. Six of the thinner names had
     none at all.
     So they are feed artifacts, not quiet bars, and nothing about them
     tracks liquidity. The original rationale here - "zero-volume bars
     repeat the old price and fake a low correlation for illiquid
     names" - is false for this universe at this bar size.
       POLICY: do NOT use Volume as a staleness test. On those dates it
       would discard a real closing move, the most informative bar of
       the day. A bar is stale only when its open, high, low and close
       all equal the previous bar's close. Stale bars become NaN and are
       NOT forward-filled. Returns are computed only between two real
       consecutive bars, and pairs are correlated on timestamps where
       BOTH stocks have a valid return (pairwise complete obs).
       Log the count of Volume == 0 bars in the fetch job anyway, as a
       diagnostic, so a change in Yahoo's feed does not pass unnoticed.
     CONFIRMED by step 0 across all 213 tickers, 306,280 bars:
       Volume == 0        217 bars   0.0709%
       stale (flat OHLC)  231 bars   0.0754%
     And the decisive number — stale rate for the 20 thinnest names by
     median traded value is 0.0941%, against 0.1075% for the 20
     thickest. Staleness is very slightly MORE common in the most liquid
     names, so it does not track illiquidity even in the direction the
     original rule assumed.
     Put the rule in a constant STALE_POLICY and note it in the UI
     footer. At 231 bars in 306,280 this choice cannot move a
     correlation; it is written down so the code does not quietly do
     something else.
  4. Log returns: np.log(price).diff(), within each session only. Do NOT
     compute a return across the overnight gap on the 15m tier (drop the
     first bar of each day). Otherwise the gap dominates.
  5. Pearson, pairwise: DataFrame.corr(min_periods=N). Set min_periods
     to 50% of the expected bars for the window; below that return NaN,
     do not return a number from a handful of points.
     Pairwise is REQUIRED, not a nicety. Step 0 measured bar counts per
     ticker of min 1,423 / median 1,438 / max 1,450 over the same 58
     sessions: most stocks are missing a bar in roughly 10 of those
     sessions, and not the same bar. Dropping any timestamp that is
     incomplete across all 213 would discard far more than it should.
  6. Daily tier: log returns of adjusted close, same pairwise logic.
  7. Save n_obs per pair so the UI can show how many points back it up.
  8. Memo by (tier, lookback, asof_date) so repeated reruns of the same
     view do not redo the work. A per-process dict, not a SQLite table —
     see the measurement in section 5: the correlation itself is 0.12s
     worst case and the bar loading is 1.45s, so what is worth memoising
     is the whole pipeline's result, in memory, for the session.
     Do not use @st.cache_data (CLAUDE.md forbids it; it conflicts with
     the SQLite caching and loses data on reboot).

Corporate actions (splits) distort returns. Within the 15m tier's 60
days the risk is small but real. Use auto_adjust=True and flag any
single-bar |return| > 20% in the log for a manual look.

7. FETCH JOB
======================================================================
File: jobs/fetch_intraday.py

This job is an OPTIMISATION, not a data-preservation requirement. It
keeps the app from refetching 60 days of bars on demand. Nothing is lost
permanently if it does not run — see the retention note in section 5.
That is a change from the 1m design, where the job was mandatory.

Run it daily after market close, around 4:30pm IST, by cron or launchd
on the user's Mac. (GitHub Actions is allowed as an alternative, but
Yahoo can rate-limit datacenter IPs; the Mac is the default. The app
only reads the files, it never calls Yahoo for this.)

  1. Download 15m for all 213 tickers — period 5d normally, 60d on a
     cold start or after a long gap — in batches of about 40-50, with a
     pause between batches.
     Keep the batching even though step 0 did all 213 in one call in
     6.0s. It is there to stay under Yahoo's undocumented limits, not to
     go faster; the speed measurement is a reason to be relaxed about
     the pauses, not to remove them.
  2. INSERT OR IGNORE into bars.
  3. On a 429 or empty result, back off, retry once, then log and
     continue. Anything still missing is recoverable on the next run,
     for up to 60 days.
  4. First run: backfill 15m for the full 60d.
  5. Prune bars older than about 65 days.
  6. (The rollup-rebuild step is gone with the rollups — see section 5.)
  7. Log: symbols fetched, bars inserted, symbols failed, runtime, and
     the Volume == 0 diagnostic from section 6 item 3.

Ticker mapping: use the F&O list from fno_sectors.json, append ".NS".
Verified 6 Oct 2026: all 213 resolve that way, BAJAJ-AUTO, GVT&D, M&M
and NAM-INDIA included — do not build a special-case mapping table for
them. Still list every symbol that returns no data and show it.

10. ACCEPTANCE CHECKS
======================================================================
Write tests in tests/test_correlation.py. All must pass.

  A. Self-correlation: every stock vs itself is 1.0 (within 1e-9).
  B. Symmetry: corr(A,B) == corr(B,A).
  C. Range: all values in [-1, 1] or NaN.
  D. Tier logic: pick_tier(1)=15m, (20)=15m, (60)=15m, (61)=1d,
     (250)=1d. Lookback 0 or negative raises an error. No input ever
     returns "1m" — that tier no longer exists.
  E. Log-return check: feed two synthetic price series with the same
     returns but different price levels. Correlation is 1.0. Feed two
     random walks with a common upward drift: price correlation is high
     but return correlation is near 0.
  F. Overnight gap: the first bar of each day is excluded from returns.
  G. Stale policy: a synthetic stock with half its bars repeating the
     previous close (flat OHLC) must not produce a correlation from
     fewer than min_periods valid pairs (returns NaN). A separate case:
     a bar with Volume == 0 whose price moved is KEPT, not dropped.
  H. Sector sanity check (needs real data): HDFCBANK vs ICICIBANK should
     have a clearly higher 20-day correlation than HDFCBANK vs a
     far-away sector (for example HDFCBANK vs SUNPHARMA). Print all
     three numbers for the user to eyeball.
  I. Cache: a second call with the same (tier, lookback, asof) does not
     recompute (check via a timer or call counter).
  J. Missing DB: the app opens without the SQLite file and shows the
     "run the fetch job" message, no crash. (Reworded 6 Oct 2026 with
     the rollups dropped — there is no fallback to fall back to.)
  K. Manual: the user picks RELIANCE, lookback 20 (15m tier) and
     lookback 120 (daily tier). Top-10 lists load in under 3 seconds
     from cache. Lookback 1 returns numbers with a visibly small n_obs
     rather than a traceback.

11. OUT OF SCOPE (later)
======================================================================
  - Rolling or time-varying correlation charts
  - Beta, PCA, clustering, hedge ratios
  - Rank (Spearman) or tail correlation
  - Replacing Yahoo with a paid or exchange feed
  - A 1-minute tier, and with it lookbacks under 5 days. Dropped 6 Oct
    2026 on two grounds. Analytical: the Epps effect (section 2, rule 4)
    biases 1m correlations toward zero, worst for exactly the illiquid
    names where a correlation estimate would be most useful. Operational:
    ~0.6 GB per 6 months, pruning logic, and a daily job that cannot be
    missed, because 1m bars older than 7 days are gone from Yahoo for
    good. Reviving it means accepting all of that again, not just adding
    a row to the tier table.
  - 1-hour bars for the long tier. Yahoo serves 1h back about 730 days,
    which would give roughly 1,750 observations over a 250-day lookback
    against daily's 250. A genuine improvement, but it needs its own
    stored tier and the rolling-730-day retention care that prices.py
    already documents. Daily first; revisit only if the daily tier's
    sample sizes prove too thin in practice.
